# Chapter 22 — The Developer's Role

A note for everyone who actually writes the code, ships the features, or
clicks accept on a suggestion.

In a system designed with humans in the loop, **you are the loop.** Not a
cog in it — the loop itself. The decisions you make in the moment are what
the system is, regardless of what the architecture diagram says.

**Every accepted suggestion is a vote.** You're telling the product —
through telemetry, behaviour, and downstream outcomes — that this kind of
output is good enough. The model doesn't have taste. You do. What you let
through becomes the standard.

**Every override is a teacher.** Saying no, editing the output, rejecting
the agent's plan — these aren't friction; they're the most valuable signal
the system gets. Treat overrides as a contribution to the product, not as a
failure of the model.

That's a serious responsibility, and it's also where the craft is. The
ergonomics of an AI workflow are downstream of how seriously the humans in
it take their own judgment. Take yours seriously, and the loop holds.
