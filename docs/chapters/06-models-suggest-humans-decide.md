# Chapter 06 — Models Suggest. Humans Decide.

This was Copilot's founding principle, and it's the line that separates an
assistant from an autopilot.

The model produces. The person decides. Notice what that *isn't*: it isn't
"the model produces and the person rubber-stamps." A rubber stamp is what
happens when the interface makes saying *no* harder than saying *yes*. That's
a design choice, and it's the wrong one.

The right design surfaces the suggestion in a form the human can genuinely
evaluate, then makes accept, edit, and reject equally cheap. Inline diffs you
can read line by line. Edits as a first-class action, not a workaround for
"the model nearly got it." A clear, frictionless override on anything the
agent does on your behalf.

Build the product so the human is always genuinely the decider — not just
nominally — and you've already done most of the work of making it
trustworthy.
