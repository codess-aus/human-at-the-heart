# Chapter 07 — Why Oversight Matters

Here's the part that's easy to skip past, so I want to slow down on it.

**Bias lives in data.** Every model is trained on something somebody chose to
collect, label, and keep. Whatever was over-represented in that data is
over-represented in the model. Whatever was missing is missing. The model
doesn't know that — it just produces fluent output either way.

**Accountability lives in people.** A model can't be held responsible. It
can't be called into a regulator's office, can't be named in a post-incident
review, can't apologise to a customer. Accountability has to land on humans —
specifically the humans who chose to deploy the system, set its policies,
and ship its outputs.

That's not a philosophical preference. It's the only design that actually
works. If accountability sits with the model, it sits nowhere, and "nobody is
responsible" is how organisations sleepwalk into harm. Oversight matters
because it's the mechanism by which responsibility stays attached to people
who can act on it.
