# Chapter 18 — Governance Without Friction

Governance fails when it's bolted on. People route around it, work despite
it, or comply just enough to keep their job. Then the safety property the
governance was meant to deliver is gone.

So the rule is *make the right thing the easy thing.* If the secure path is
also the fastest path, you don't have to enforce it.

**Policies as code.** Write the rules in a form the system itself can apply —
not a PDF a human is supposed to remember at 5pm on a Friday. The same
mindset we already use for infrastructure: declarative, versioned, reviewed.

**Defaults that protect.** The unconfigured behaviour should be the safer
behaviour. Customers who do nothing should still be in a defensible position.
Configuration is for unlocking *more* freedom, not for reaching a baseline of
sanity.

**Telemetry that respects privacy.** You can't improve what you can't see,
and you can't be trusted if what you see crosses the line. Collect what's
genuinely needed; aggregate and anonymise aggressively; be explicit about
what you keep and for how long.

Done well, governance disappears into the product — and that's the
compliment, not the failure mode.
