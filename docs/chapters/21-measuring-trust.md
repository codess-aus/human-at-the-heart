# Chapter 21 — Measuring Trust

You can't manage what you don't measure, and most teams measure the wrong
things. Here's a shortlist that's served us well.

**Acceptance rate *and* edit rate.** Acceptance alone is misleading —
suggestions that get accepted and then heavily edited are not really
"accepted." Tracking both together tells you whether the model is *useful*
or merely *agreeable*.

**Override frequency.** How often are users hitting the escape hatch?
Spikes are signals: a deployment regressed, a new cohort is underserved, a
prompt is being misused. Quietly trending up is the most important version
to catch.

**Reported harms, time-to-resolve.** A live channel for users to flag harm,
and an internal commitment to a turnaround time. If reports go into a void,
trust evaporates. If the team is visibly responsive, trust compounds.

**Outcome parity across cohorts.** Bring the inclusivity principle back into
daily measurement. Quality, latency, harm-rate — sliced by the dimensions
that matter to your users. If parity slips, treat it like a production
incident, not a research curiosity.

Four numbers. Put them on a dashboard the team actually looks at.
