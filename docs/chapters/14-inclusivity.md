# Chapter 14 — Inclusivity

The third principle is the one most likely to be treated as a marketing
position rather than an engineering requirement. It's both — but let's stay
on the engineering side.

Inclusivity in an AI system means the product works for the actual
distribution of people who will use it, not the slice that happened to be
loudest in the training set. That's a measurable property. You measure it by
splitting your evaluations along the dimensions that matter — language,
domain, experience level, disability, geography — and looking at whether
quality holds up across cohorts, or whether it quietly collapses on the
ones that are hardest to see in aggregate metrics.

When quality *does* collapse on a cohort, you don't ship the average. You
fix the cohort, or you scope the product so it doesn't make promises it
can't keep.

Built *with* the people it serves means something concrete: representation
in the dataset, representation in the eval set, representation on the team
making the calls. Inclusivity isn't a value statement — it's whose voice is
in the room when the trade-off is being made.
