# Chapter 16 — Trust, Shipped as Product

Principles only matter if they show up in the surface area users actually
touch. Here's how that lands inside Copilot.

**Code referencing — transparency on training data.** When a suggestion
resembles existing public code, Copilot can tell you so, and point to where.
That's transparency translated into a feature — not a footnote on the
website, but an inline signal at the moment you'd want it.

**Content exclusions — IP control.** Organisations can declare repositories
or paths that must never be used as context, and Copilot will respect that
boundary. The trust commitment is being honoured by the product itself, not
by a policy document people hope is being followed.

**Audit logs — accountability.** Every relevant action is recorded in a
form that admins, auditors, and incident responders can use. When something
goes wrong, there is a trail. When something goes right, there's evidence of
that too.

**Policy controls — the enterprise.** Administrators can shape Copilot's
behaviour to match their compliance posture, not the other way around.
Defaults are safe, and the dial is in the customer's hands.

Trust, in other words, isn't a slogan in the marketing copy. It's a row in
the feature list.
